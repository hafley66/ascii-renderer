## Problem/Context
- The encoder paid a fixed per-cell scan on every frame (about 27 ns/cell, 1,992 us for a
  completely unchanged frame at 366x199) because it converted every cell and let Ratatui
  diff the whole grid. Step 2's lever is that scan.
- Per-frame cell churn in the animation loop, measured with the ruler at dt 0.06 and
  default knobs over 16 modes: 0.0 percent (snakes, chladni), 0.3 (flux), 0.5 (astrolabe),
  1.8 (tideglass), 2.8 (arboretum), 3.8 (cosmograph), 3.9 (prismata), 7.5
  (gem-aetherium-2), 7.9 (illuminarium), 10.0 (poincare), 12.8 (delta), 29.1 (hyperloom),
  29.9 (mahoraga-5), 34.4 (pendulum-wave), 76.6 (terminal-stress). The median mode moves
  4 percent of its cells per step, and four of sixteen move more than a quarter. Both
  regimes matter; the user is right that the churn-heavy one is the one to get right.
- Revision 2 (per-changed-cell reference refresh) was byte-identical but made the
  churn-heavy modes slower than HEAD: pendulum-wave 2,342 to 2,856 us at 34 percent
  churn. Isolation: building the same tree with the refresh removed (variant E, wrong
  bytes, timing only) took pendulum-wave's `convert` from 1,548 to 1,179 us, so the
  48-byte copy costs about 370 us there, 14 to 18 ns per changed cell. Replacing
  `clone_from` with a field-wise copy (`set_symbol` plus fg/bg/modifier, variant F) was
  measured and is worse (3,013 us), so this is memory traffic, not a `CompactString`
  pathology.
- Two earlier shapes were rejected on guard evidence: `Skip` with the buffer flip emitted
  45,510 bytes where HEAD emits 25,421 (because `ratatui::buffer::Cell` equality includes
  `diff_option`, and a flip leaves skipped cells holding a value from two frames back),

  change-back sequence.

## Solution/Approach
- The final shape keeps two 12-byte-per-cell caches of the last adapted frame: the raw
  `Cell` shadow (did this cell move at all) and the `AdaptedCell` cache (does the
  terminal need to change). The second cache is what decides emission, so no 48-byte
  Ratatui cell is ever copied, and no cell is compared by Ratatui at all: every cell ends
  up either `Skip` or `AlwaysUpdate`, and `AlwaysUpdate` bypasses the equality check.
- `previous` survives only for the wide-glyph columns: Ratatui reads the cell it is
  replacing (`previous_width`, `bg`, `modifier`, and `symbol()` of trailing neighbours)
  to clear the columns a wide glyph reserved. Only those cells are put in step, from the
  adapted cache's previous value, and `synced_wide` remembers where a wide glyph is still
  parked so it is put back in step again before the next time it is read.
- Full repaints mark every cell `AlwaysUpdate` and refresh both caches for every cell,
  which is why they are about 300 us more expensive than HEAD's; there is at most one per
  session plus one per resize, strategy change, pause or unpresented frame, and the live
  numbers include it.
- Measurement discipline that held up: three binaries copied out of the build directory
  (the test binary's file name hash depends on crate metadata, not on source) and run
  alternately in one session, 11 reps, median plus min, with byte, changed-cell and
  skipped-cell columns asserted equal across revisions on every row.

## Key Insights
- `ratatui::buffer::Cell` has a manual `PartialEq` that includes `diff_option`, so
  diff-option bookkeeping is part of correctness, not bookkeeping. `CellDiffOption::Skip`
  is honoured before the comparison, and `AlwaysUpdate` skips the comparison entirely.
- Wide glyphs are not theoretical here: cosmograph, `_34_gemini_astrolabe` and
  `_50_gem_aetherium_2` draw the zodiac signs, thunderhead draws `〜`, and cli_basic draws
  `技`. The reason `previous` cannot simply be dropped is a single ratatui branch that
  clears the reserved columns of a replaced wide glyph, and every read it performs is
  reachable from the emitted cells only.
- What the win actually is: skipping the two `ratatui_color` conversions (about 19 ns per
  cell) and, in the adapted revision, skipping the diff's content comparisons (about
  7.5 ns per cell) as well. What it costs is 24 bytes of cache stores per cell per frame.
  That is why revision 2's 48-byte reference copy ate the win at high churn while the
  adapted cache does not.
- The two failure modes to test for, both found by tests rather than reasoning: a cell
  that changes, rests, then returns to its earlier value (must be repainted), and a
  resting cell whose change is invisible (a blank cell's foreground, or adjacent RGB
  levels collapsing to one palette index; must emit nothing).
- Measurement traps hit and paid for: single-shot probe numbers on a loaded host run 15
  to 40 percent high, so keep the min column; the test binary's name hash is derived from
  crate metadata so a rebuild overwrites the same path; the older probe binary in
  `/tmp/probe-A` has one fewer column than the newer ones, which silently shifts any
  column-indexed parse.
- `Cell::copy` alternatives measured and rejected: `Cell::clone_from` is the fastest way
  to snapshot a Ratatui cell; a field-wise copy is worse; a `mem::swap` of the two cells
  moves the same 48 bytes.
