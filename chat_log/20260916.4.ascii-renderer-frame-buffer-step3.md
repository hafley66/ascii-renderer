# Session: ascii-renderer-frame-buffer-step3

## Goal
Continue the perf queue from `perf/16_LARGE_RENDER_PLAN.md` after steps 1 and 2
landed. This session took the plan's next unlanded step, **step 3, buffer hygiene
on the frame path**, and landed it: the encoder no longer copies and revalidates
each frame's ANSI payload into a `String`, and the live loop no longer memmoves the
composed frame buffer twice per frame to insert its prefixes. Unlike step 2, the
measured win is small and payload-dependent, and the receipt says so rather than
repeating the plan's projection.

## Problem/Context
- The encoder's per-frame tail was `output.clear(); output.push_str(std::str::from_utf8(&self.bytes).expect(..))`,
  a whole-payload UTF-8 validation plus a whole-payload copy, and `src/morph.rs`
  inserted `\x1b[?2026h` and `\x18...` with `insert_str(0, ..)`, each a memmove of
  the ~376 to 601 KB composed frame.
- Live prismata frames at 366x199 with all knobs at max are 67 percent churn: 48,775
  changed cells of 72,834, `frame_buffer.len()` 376 to 601 KB, payload about 480 KB.
- The trap this session hit and paid for: a first micro-bench built its payload with
  14 percent non-ASCII and reported 178 us for the removed work, which predicted a
  visible live `emit` drop that did not appear. Real art payloads are 0.2 percent
  non-ASCII (`terminal.ansi.gz` of the prismata case: 12,352,446 bytes, 8,509
  three-byte leads), so validation stays on the fast path and the removed work is
  18.1 us. The live column was right and the synthetic payload was wrong.
- The live `emit` column is also noisy on a shared host: `render_us` moved 40 percent
  between the adapted and step-3 runs (1,107 to 1,548) while `emit` stayed flat, so
  live numbers here are identity evidence, not size evidence. The probe table is the
  size.
- Measurement discipline that held up again: one binary per revision copied out of
  the build directory (the test binary's file name hash depends on crate metadata,
  not source), alternated in one session, medians plus min, byte/changed/skipped
  columns asserted equal on every row.

## Solution/Approach
- Give the buffer to the caller and let the encoder append. Ordering makes the
  prefixes free: everything the terminal must see before the payload is written
  before `encode` runs, so no finished frame is ever moved. That also deletes a
  field, a `String` round-trip and the UTF-8 validation, with no new state.
- Keep the claim proportional to the evidence: this is a small, mechanism-explained
  win (glyph density decides it) plus a strict simplification, not the half
  millisecond the plan hoped for. The plan and the receipt both now say that.

## Key Insights
- UTF-8 validation is only expensive when the bytes are not ASCII. Every estimate of
  "the copy costs the payload size" has to be conditioned on the art's glyph density,
  and the fast path is why this step is 1 percent on prismata and 8 percent on
  illuminarium.
- A synthetic payload is not a stand-in for a real one. The 178 us figure was
  produced by a bench whose only defect was its non-ASCII fraction; measuring the
  removed work on bytes taken from the live stream is what settled it.
- Prefix insertion is the wrong shape for a frame buffer. Writing the prefix before
  the payload removes the memmove without any reserve/rotate trick and makes the
  stream order explicit in the source.
- Live E2E numbers on a shared host cannot resolve sub-100 us encode differences;
  cross-revision byte identity at a common `frame_index` is the strong live claim,
  and the in-process probe is the size claim.
- The three guard levels for anything on this path are: the probe's byte/changed/
  skipped columns across modes, the 60-frame gem comparator tuple, and shared-frame
  live identity.
