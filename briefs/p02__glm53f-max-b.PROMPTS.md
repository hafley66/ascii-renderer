## Artistic brief

Take the art of one place and rebuild it as geometry that moves. Choose one region: Japan, India, China, Russia, Europe, Africa, or Iran. Extract two things from it: the geometry that only it uses, and one famous pattern tradition whose rules you can implement. Blend them algorithmically into one original animated ASCII art mode with a strong, immediately recognizable composition, using multidimensional geometry, fractal growth, recursive systems, simulated depth, or another bounded generative system as the core structure. The pattern must be generated by rules, never stamped from a picture, and the region's geometry must be the structure the motion flows through. The result must be exciting and a joy to look at.

Candidate pairings, to spark the choice rather than assign it:

- Japan: ukiyo-e wave and cloud linework, asanoha and seigaiha lattices, roof-curve asymmetry
- India: rangoli and kolam knot grids, mandala radial symmetry, gopuram silhouette
- China: meander and cloud-scroll bands, lattice window geometry, layered mountain valleys
- Russia: khokhloma curves, constructivist diagonals, onion dome rooflines
- Europe: rose-window wheel geometry, gothic tracery, art nouveau whiplash, damask grids
- Africa: adire and kente strip math, kuba cloth interlace, sahel adobe and dune geometry
- Iran: girih tile tessellation, muqarnas vaulting, arabesque interlace, carpet field logic

Do not render flags, maps, monuments, or literal silhouettes. Extract the spatial logic and the pattern grammar, then compose. Name the chosen region and pattern tradition in your plan and in the mode's help line. Different seeds should read as different works in the same tradition.

Interpret multidimensional broadly. Available dimensions include:

- 3D or 4D geometry projected into terminal space
- recursive depth and self-similarity
- time and phase
- branch lineage
- environmental vector fields
- energy or resource flow
- occlusion and rendered depth
- multiple interacting scales
- topology that remains stable while its projection changes

Choose a specific visual thesis and carry it through every decision. Possible ingredients include recursive trees, space colonization, L-systems, strange attractors, phyllotaxis, reaction-diffusion surfaces, gravitational distortion, mycelial networks, cathedral geometry, astronomical diagrams, fossils, nervous systems, storms, or invented organisms. These are ingredients, not requirements.

The result must have:

- a legible large-scale silhouette
- deliberate negative space
- meaningful detail at multiple scales
- coherent glyph vocabulary
- intentional color hierarchy
- deterministic structural variation between seeds
- motion that reveals or transforms the underlying system
- a composed frame at 80x24

Avoid filling the canvas with statistically uniform noise. Every visible layer must contribute structure, depth, atmosphere, motion, or focus.

## Continuous system and emergence

Build one coupled generative system. High variability must arise from continuous functions, interacting state, feedback, projection, and seeded initial conditions. Do not implement a menu of unrelated algorithms selected by enums, integer variant indices, large match statements, or threshold bands. Do not render several independent modes from the same inputs and merely composite or crossfade their outputs.

Represent the artistic control space primarily with continuous values. Nearby parameter values should usually produce nearby structures and motion. Moving a control through its range should deform, fold, grow, couple, bifurcate, or reorganize the same system.

Useful continuous operations include:

- interpolation and smoothstep curves
- continuous vector and scalar fields
- signed distance combinations and soft minima
- angular interpolation
- curl or domain-warped noise
- coupled oscillators
- reaction and diffusion
- attraction, repulsion, and occupancy gradients
- continuously weighted branching probabilities
- energy transport and conservation
- feedback between density, curvature, growth, and light

Discrete topology changes may emerge from continuous conditions. Make their causes visible and deterministic. Avoid arbitrary artistic thresholds that switch between prebuilt visual recipes.

At least two subsystems must interact bidirectionally or through shared state. An example is growth changing an occupancy field while the occupancy field redirects later growth. Other examples include projected density bending trajectories, light driving growth while branches occlude light, or particles depositing a field that changes subsequent particle motion.

Expose a compact continuous parameter vector through `Mode::params`. Every primary parameter must influence the common system rather than choose a renderer. Demonstrate smooth traversal through the parameter space by inspecting a sweep at five ordered values, including adjacent middle values where continuity is easiest to judge.
